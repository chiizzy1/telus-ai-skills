# Scenario Catalog

Use with `../SKILL.md`. The guideline's six offensive scenarios and four not-offensive carve-outs, in full, with the rule each one contributes. `decision-matrix.md` is the fast path; this file is what that matrix is built from, and it is where you check an unusual pair.

The guideline closes both lists with the same instruction: do not limit yourself to these, use judgement. The scenarios are the shape of the harm, not an exhaustive list of it.

## Offensive scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Kids and family query against an adult-themed ad

A user searching for a child- or family-focused place is very surprised to see an ad for an adult-themed business.

Kids/family covers the obvious words (`kid`, `school`, `preschool`, `kids birthday party venue`) **and places whose primary audience is children or families even when the query says nothing about children**. The guideline names `Legoland` and `Great Wolf Lodge`. This is the part that gets missed: if you do not know what a queried place is, look it up before deciding it is a generic query.

Adult-themed, per the guideline: adult entertainment such as a nightclub, bars and alcohol including a wine bar, brewing company or alcohol retailer, plastic surgery, casino, tattoo, smoke shop, and anything else your judgement puts in the same company.

Worked: `kids birthday party venue` → Empire City Casino is **Majority**. `preschoool` → Voodoo Brewing Co. is **Majority**.

### Scenario 2 — Opposing-purpose mismatch

A user searching for a place with a specific purpose or point of view is offended by an ad promoting the opposite purpose.

The guideline's examples: `vegetarian` → butcher shop or meat market; `funeral` → bar, gambling or adult entertainment; `animal shelter` → hunting outfitter.

**The carve-out inside this scenario:** specific restaurants and cuisines are not offensive for vegetarian or vegan queries, because any restaurant can provide vegetarian-friendly food. Only meat-specific businesses count.

Worked: `vegetarian restaurants` → 3 Arrows Boutique Butcher is **Some**. `animal shelter` → Backwoods Hunting Outfitters is **Some**.

### Scenario 3 — Religion-sensitive pairs

A user searching for a religious place is offended by an ad that violates that religion's prohibitions: alcohol, gambling, tobacco, or adult content.

For dietary-sensitive religions, which the guideline lists as Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism and Buddhism, meat-specific places may also be offensive. Butcher shops and meat markets, not restaurants.

Two rules ride on this scenario, and both cut against over-rating:

- `halal food` → a restaurant that is not halal-specific is **not offensive**. `halal food` → a butcher shop **is** offensive.
- **A mismatch between religions is not offensive.** A mosque shown on a `church` query is fine. Only the queried religion's own prohibitions matter.

Worked: `mosque` → Total Wine & More is **Majority**.

### Scenario 4 — Promotes unsafe behavior that could harm the user

A user searching for a place may be harmed by an ad for another place whose typical use directly conflicts with their safety in that context.

The guideline's case is driving: someone searching a car rental such as Hertz, a gas station, or a driving school should not be shown an alcohol-related ad or anything else that impairs driving.

The principle generalizes to any query that puts the user in a situation where the advertised activity would be dangerous. The test is whether the two activities are unsafe *in combination*, not whether either is unsafe alone.

Worked: `car rental near me` → The Tap Room (bar) is **Majority**.

### Scenario 5 — Medical and health vulnerability

A user searching for medical care or treatment is harmed by an ad that either **(a) undermines their health goal** or **(b) preys on their vulnerable moment**.

The guideline's examples: `hospital` or `prenatal` → an alcohol, tobacco or gambling ad; `cancer` or `oncology` → a funeral or end-of-life ad; `sexual health clinic` → an adult-entertainment ad.

**The recovery rule, stated separately:** if the query is about addiction recovery and the ad promotes the addictive substance or activity, mark it offensive. This is the most clear-cut pairing on the whole task.

Worked: `alcohol addiction treatment center` → Wiley's Liquor Store is **Majority**. `urgent care` → Wiley's Liquor Store is **Majority**. `cancer treatment center` → Restland Funeral Home is **Majority**.

### Scenario 6 — Ambiguous query surfaces a sensitive interpretation

A user searching a benign term that shares a name with a sensitive place is surprised and alarmed to see an ad for the sensitive reading. The guideline calls this context collapse, and says it erodes trust.

The guideline's examples: `death valley`, meaning the national park, shown a funeral-home ad; `target range`, meaning a shooting range, shown a Target retail ad.

The tell is that the ad looks matched to a **word** in the query rather than to what the user meant.

## Not-offensive carve-outs

The guideline is as explicit about what is not offensive as about what is. All four of these exist to stop raters marking irrelevance as offence.

### No relationship, not relevant

A pair may be odd or surprising, but if it does not create an upsetting experience or a sensitive mismatch, it is not offensive. The guideline's example is `hospital` → a grocery store ad.

### Non-adult query plus adult ad, with no mismatch or vulnerability

`Hotel`, `McDonald's` or `Starbucks` shown a casino or bar ad is **Not Offensive**. A generic query seeing an adult-category ad is not, by itself, offensive. Judge case by case.

### Competitors in the same category

`McDonald's` → a Burger King ad is **Not Offensive**. Competitive overlap is a relevance matter and has nothing to do with this task.

### User already indicating adult or sensitive intent

A user who searched for a bar or an adult venue is not shocked to see a similar ad. They asked for this category.

## When you cannot tell

The guideline addresses this directly. Occasionally you will not be able to tell whether the majority or some of the population would find a pairing offensive.

In that case: **research the query and the business**, opening the Maps URL and the website URL, give an educated response, and explain the choice in the comment.

Note what it does not say. It does not offer an abstain option, and it does not tell you to default to Some. Research, decide, and say what the decision rested on.

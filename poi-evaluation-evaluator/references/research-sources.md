# Research Sources (§2)

Three tiers, consulted **in order of preference**. The middle tier has no equivalent in
the Maps skill.

## Tier 1 — Official (§2.1.2)

**Official websites.** Owned and maintained by the POI. Recognisable by a distinct
domain, corporate design and logo, and customised content — contact details, history,
the services offered. The address is usually in the footer or a Contact Us section; for
chains, in the store locator.

**Official social media pages** — only where the POI lacks an official website, and
only when **claimed by the business and updated within the past 12 calendar months**.

How to tell a claimed page from an unclaimed one:

| Signal | Official | Unofficial |
|---|---|---|
| Posts | Updates and photos posted **by the business** | Only member-contributed content |
| Badge | Verified or claimed by owner | None |
| URL | Custom extension, usually the POI's name — `facebook.com/LaTaqSF` | Random digits — `facebook.com/pages/Equine-Calculator/857469375913?ref=ts` |
| Prompts | No "Claim this Page" link | "Is this your business?" present |

> **Do not log in.** Use only what is visible logged out. If a page requires a login,
> it cannot be used as an official source.

## Tier 2 — Authoritative (§2.1.3)

Used when official sources are unavailable, **or to confirm details official sources do
not provide**:

- Postal authorities — for postal codes and locality names
- Government maps — land registry, cadastre, GIS
- Government business registries

## Tier 3 — Reliable (§2.1.4)

Only after the first two are exhausted, and then **rate on consensus among several**:

- Crowdsourced user review sites
- Unofficial social media pages
- Articles in primary publications — outlets doing their own reporting

## Never — unreliable

Data aggregators, spammy or unofficial directory sources. **Consensus among unreliable
sources is disregarded entirely.**

## Tools that support research but are not sources

**Search engines** locate official sources. §2.1.1.1 is explicit:

> SERPs should be used to locate official sources, but information from search engine
> results pages **should not be used to corroborate POI data or confirm a POI's
> open/closed status.**

**Street imagery** may corroborate official, authoritative or reliable sources, and in
some cases stands as evidence where nothing else exists. Always seek the most recent
drive available, and look from several positions — different roads and lanes, since the
kerbside lane often shows the clearest storefront while the opposite lane may carry a
more recent capture. **Historical imagery can support an inference of closure**: a POI
visible at the address in older imagery and absent from newer imagery suggests it is no
longer active (§3.2.1.2.3).

## Category exceptions (§2.2)

| POI type | Approved substitute |
|---|---|
| **EV chargers** | PlugShare, or the Alternative Fuels Data Center in the US, count as authoritative-equivalent. Consult street imagery too |
| **Bike share** | Consensus across multiple street imagery resources; where the market has only one, use what is available |
| **Bus stops** | Names are often a road, intersection or place name. **Check the category** when the name looks like an address — `2138 Shopiere Rd` is a bus stop |
| **In-store kiosks** | Treat as POIs within POIs; confirm through the kiosk's official site or the host POI's |

## The verification standard

> **§2:** If more information is shown in the listing than on the official website, the
> additional information must be verified through official or authoritative sources.

And the shared TELUS quality gate: **never report a verification you did not perform.**
Saying a page could not be read is always better than implying you read it. A snippet
locates a source; only the opened source substantiates a rating.

`Can't Verify` means the evidence does not exist. It does not mean your fetch failed.
Before submitting one, run one targeted search for another source — a host your
tooling cannot fetch is still reachable through search, and what it shows is evidence.

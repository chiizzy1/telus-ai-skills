# Comment Style

Use with `../SKILL.md`. This file is the authoritative source for how a Broad Match comment is written.

The guideline requires a comment in one case explicitly: when the keyword, expansion or both are in a language not used in your market, you translate and **leave a note in the Comments box explaining what you did**. Write a comment on every rating anyway. The Explanation column of the guideline's own tables is the model, and matching it is the fastest way to read as consistent with the rubric.

## The formula

Two sentences. One is often enough for a Good rating.

1. **Name the operation or the relationship.** What was done to the term, or how the two things relate. A plural, a translation, a competitor, a feature of the other app.
2. **Say what that does to the intent.** Shared, unchanged, or different, and what each intent is when they differ.

For a Bad rating, name **both** intents. "Different intents" on its own tells the reviewer nothing; "keyword is a scanning app and the expansion is an editor app" tells them everything.

Write in English regardless of market. When you translated something, say so.

## Worked examples by rating

**Good, a lexical operation**

> `The expansion is a plural form of the keyword. It does not change the intent.`

> `A space has been added that is not part of the app's name, but the name is still very recognizable.`

> `This change in word order has not changed the meaning.`

**Good, a translation, with the required note**

> `The keyword and expansion are translations of each other, both meaning cash transfer. Translated using an online tool to confirm.`

**Good, words added or removed**

> `The word "free" has been removed from the expansion, and the meaning and intention stay the same.`

**Good, former app name**

> `Both refer to the same app, since the keyword is the former name of the expansion.`

**Acceptable, competitors**

> `Both are food delivery apps and compete in the same space, so the keyword and expansion share the same intent.`

**Acceptable, brand and its function**

> `Nike is well known for its running app, so the keyword and expansion share the same running intent.`

**Acceptable, non-brand terms with one goal**

> `Both the keyword and the expansion have the same intent of diet-related apps.`

**Acceptable, broad keyword covering a specific expansion**

> `The keyword is generic enough to cover kids' games, and toddlers are included in that intent.`

**Bad, different intents in the same category**

> `The keyword is a sports e-commerce app and the expansion is a sports broadcasting app, so the intents differ despite the shared category.`

**Bad, two products of one brand**

> `Both belong to Amazon, but the keyword is for grocery delivery and the expansion is for a home assistant.`

**Bad, specific keyword against a broad expansion**

> `The keyword is very specific to shooting games and the expansion is a generic query for online games.`

**Bad, a keyword too broad to carry intent**

> `Both share the intent of apps, but the expansion is specific to streaming, so the two do not share an intent.`

## Rules

- **Two sentences maximum.** One for a simple Good.
- **Name the category's substance**, not its label. Write "the expansion is a plural form", not "Singular/plural category".
- **On Bad, name both intents.** One each side, in plain words.
- **Say which side is which.** Use "the keyword" and "the expansion". This task is directional and the reviewer needs to know which way you read it.
- **Never use em dashes.** Commas, colons, periods or parentheses.
- **No AI filler.** No "delve", "moreover", "furthermore", "it is worth noting".
- **No guideline references.** No section or category numbers, no "per the guidelines".
- **No mention of tools**, except the one case the guideline requires: say that you translated a term.
- **Do not restate the two terms.** The reviewer can see them. Give the relationship.

## Anti-patterns

**Asserting a shared category as if it settled the rating.** The guideline rejects this in almost every Bad explanation.

- Wrong: *"Both are sports apps, so they share intent."*
- Right: *"The keyword is a sports e-commerce app and the expansion is a sports broadcasting app, so the intents differ despite the shared category."*

**Naming no intent on a Bad rating.**

- Wrong: *"The keyword and expansion have different intents."*
- Right: *"The keyword is a specific diet and the expansion is a generic term for cookbooks."*

**Reasoning from appearance instead of intent.** That is the Close Variants question, not this one.

- Wrong: *"The two terms look nothing alike, so they cannot match."*
- Right: *"Both the keyword and the expansion have the same intent of fitness apps."*

**Ignoring direction.** The same two phrases can rate differently depending on which is the keyword.

- Wrong: *"Toddler games and learning games for kids both cover young children."*
- Right: *"The keyword is specific to toddlers and the expansion is broader, covering all kids, so the keyword's intent does not reach it."*

**Claiming a functional overlap you did not verify.** Whether a brand offers a function is a fact to check, not to assume.

- Wrong: *"Adidas presumably offers a running app like other sports brands."*
- Right: *"Nike is well known for its running app, so the keyword and expansion share the same running intent."*

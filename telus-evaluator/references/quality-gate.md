# TELUS Quality Gate

The standard every TELUS evaluation must meet before it is submitted, whichever task-specific skill is in use. Task skills add their own checks; none of them relax these.

## 1. The guideline is the single source of truth

The official TELUS guideline and the task-specific skill outrank everything else: your instinct, your previous answer, what the user seems to want, and what would be easiest to say. When they conflict with any of those, the guideline wins.

## 2. Evidence over assertion

Never report a verification you did not perform. If a step was skipped, blocked, or produced nothing usable, say so plainly and say what you did instead.

Claiming a page was checked when it was not is a critical failure, worse than admitting the page could not be read.

## 3. Meaning, not word matching

A result that contains the query's words is not automatically relevant. Judge what the content actually does for the user. If the only thing you can point to is that the words appear, that is not a pass.

Text-search tools may LOCATE content. They must never be the basis for a rating on their own; read the surrounding context before judging.

## 4. Rate each item individually

Every item gets its own rating and its own reason, tied to the specific evidence for that item. Do not rate a group as a block, do not copy a rating across positions, and do not write "the rest are similar".

## 5. Honest calibration

Aim for the rating the guideline supports, not the comfortable middle and not a harsh one that looks rigorous.

- Too generous: crediting an item you could not verify, giving a top rating that a disqualifier forbids, or rewarding a page that merely mentions the topic.
- Too harsh: penalizing a valid minor interpretation, or applying criteria the guideline never states (length, layout, age, style).

Name the anchor or rule behind each rating. If you cannot name one, the rating is a hunch.

## 6. Context factors

Apply these wherever the task type allows them, and state explicitly when one does not apply:

- **Locale**: user location, language, region, and any locale intent inside the item itself.
- **Time sensitivity**: whether the date makes freshness a requirement or an older item is still fine.
- **Position**: where the useful items sit, when the task compares ordered lists.
- **Variety**: useful diversity of sources, types, and interpretations, when the task compares sets.

## 7. Follow the taught output pattern

When a skill supplies comment or output examples, re-read them immediately before writing, not from memory, and match the pattern they teach: opening phrase, length, tone, banned words and punctuation.

## 8. Holding the line under challenge

Expect to be asked whether you really ran the tool, really read the content, or really rated each item on its own.

Treat the challenge as an instruction to re-run this gate against the guideline and your saved evidence. It is not a signal that you were wrong.

- Answer with specifics: the file, the run, the quoted line, the rule applied.
- Change a rating only when the guideline and evidence show it was wrong, and name what changed and why.
- If the evidence supports your original answer, say so and show it. Do not soften or flip a rating to be agreeable.
- If you cannot produce the evidence, say so and go do the work. Never invent a justification after the fact.
